Grace keeps us SAFE!

19 August 2026

Live under it and flee from the curse of the law.

God gave me such a revelation this week about covenant. The covenant we choose to live under is the one whose blessings or curses we come under. When we mix the Old and New Covenants, we automatically put ourselves under the curses that would come on those who live under the laws of the Old.

When life is tough and we search our hearts and lives to see if there could be any open door to the enemy that we are unaware of, we sometimes step into the place of fear and control, not faith and freedom. We move from Grace to Law, to cover our bases. Giving to God is never about what we will get in return. (I’m not referring to giving by faith with an expectation of a yield, but giving under compulsion out of fear that we might come under the curse if we don’t). If we do, we base our giving on works and it becomes a transaction upon which we expect God to act. The root motivation of that kind of giving is fear, not faith.

I’ve been thinking a lot about covenant over the last few days. Marriage covenant is about growing and journeying through lack and abundance, sickness and health, youth and maturity, sorrow and joy. It’s about living life together through thick and thin, and knowing each other in each of those places… and sticking it out together. In the valleys and on the mountaintops. That’s what marriage covenant is about. It’s not about living the ideal life where everything is stable and good, and things are held together peacefully and harmoniously. Where memories are built, holidays are enjoyed, pictures are taken and stories are told from adventures spent together. That’s only a small benefit of the whole. But it’s not what covenant is.

With God it is the same. He is here to go through life’s journey with us. Through the thick and thin, the tough and easy. In those different places, we find Him and He becomes known to us. This is what He longs for more than anything. For the First Commandment is to love Him with all our hearts, but how can we do so if we don’t know Him? We restrict Him, put Him in a box, and tell Him who He must be for us. He is God, and as such, He is a prayer-answering God. That’s His function and His responsibility. However, in covenant, that’s not what it’s about. It’s about relationship. It’s about intimacy. It’s about knowing Him and being known.

When we take the time to know Him, we realise that He delights in doing wonders and miracles. That is His nature. He doesn’t want to make our lives safe and secure in things, where we don’t need His intervention. Where we are so independent from Him that, even in the covenant, we live as strangers on our terms, never really allowing Him to be Him. Never sharing in the joy that He feels when He shows up in His Glory, doing what He does best: MIRACLES!

We abort that process when we rush ahead to make things work. We see that in the life of Abraham when he tried to intervene, rather than wait on God for His perfect timing. This last season of trial the Remnant have been going through has been a valley season where God has made Himself known as our Comforter in the place of lack and sorrow. He is faithful to continue to make Himself known as our Saviour.

What God has wrought in us is courage to trust and endure. Humility to surrender and obey. Now He is going to reveal to us His nature as our Kinsman-Redeemer, who will do miracles and transform our valley of sorrow into joy. However, He is calling us to stay in the place of Grace, in the place where He is known. Flee, with all your might, from those who want to put you into bondage. From those who speak law and its curse over you. You are under the New Covenant of Grace where Jesus has paid the price for your freedom. Your past, present and future is in His Hands, secure and safe, because He loves you. Choose Grace, because under it, there is no curse.
